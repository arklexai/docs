Scenarios define the simulated users that interact with your agent during a simulation. Each scenario specifies what the user wants (the **goal**), who they are (the **user profile**), and what they know (**knowledge**). A scenario can also carry **assertions**, which decide whether a conversation passes.

<Note>
  New here? The [Quickstart](/quickstart) walks you through building your first scenarios end to end. This page is the full reference.
</Note>

---

## Scenario groups

Scenarios live in **scenario groups**. A group is a collection of related scenarios, such as all scenarios for a product area, support topic, or user segment. For example, a "Core Support" group could cover balances, transfers, and card issues.

Go to **Scenarios** in the sidebar to see every group in your organization. Each card shows the group name, when it was created, and who created it.

You create a group before you add scenarios to it:

1. Click **Create group**.
2. Enter a **Name**. If you leave it blank, the group is called "Untitled group".
3. Click **Create**, then open the group.

Inside a group, you can:

- Rename the group with the pencil icon next to its name.
- Click **Download all** to export the group's scenarios as a JSON file.
- Click **Add Scenarios** to build, import, or generate more scenarios.
- Switch between the **Scenarios** tab (saved scenarios) and the **Drafts** tab (work in progress).

To delete a group, open the menu on its card and click **Delete**. You can't delete a group while any of its scenarios is used by a simulation. Deleting a group also deletes its unsaved drafts.

## Anatomy of a scenario

Click a scenario in a group to open it. A scenario has these parts:

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **User Profile** | Who the simulated user is: background, knowledge level, tone, and constraints. It can include a customer segment and attributes. |
| **User Goal** | What the simulated user is trying to accomplish, written from their perspective. For example: "You think there's a charge on your account you don't recognize and you want it reversed." |
| **User Goal Checklist** | Up to {checklistItems} items, each marked **Required** or **Optional**, that tell the simulated user when it's done. |
| **Assertions** | The checks that decide whether a conversation passes. See [Assertions](#assertions). |
| **Agent Context** | What the agent is and what it does, from the simulated user's point of view. |
| **Knowledge** | Up to {knowledgePerScenario} passages the simulated user can draw on. |

The knowledge passages are copied into the scenario when you create it. Later changes to your knowledge base, including deleting an item, don't change saved scenarios.

<Tip>
  Knowledge grounds what the **simulated user** knows and asks about, not the agent's answers. It also shapes the generated checklist and assertions. Grounding scenarios in the same material your agent uses is a good way to test whether the agent stays faithful to it.
</Tip>

## Assertions

Assertions are the checks that decide whether a conversation passes. Three things shape a conversation's result, and only assertions decide it:

- **Assertions** grade the agent. A conversation passes only if every assertion passes. A scenario with no assertions shows **Not evaluated**.
- The **user goal checklist** tells the simulated user when it's done, so it controls when the conversation ends. It doesn't grade the agent. For example, an agent that correctly refuses to cancel a shipped order passes, even though the user wanted it cancelled.
- **Metrics** add scores from the LLM judge but don't change the result. See [Metrics](/metrics).

Use assertions for must-have behaviors, and metrics for quality you want to track over time.

The assistant writes assertions for each scenario it drafts. To add or change them, open the scenario, click **Edit**, and click **Add assertion** in the **Assertions** section. There are three kinds:

| Kind | What it checks |
| --- | --- |
| **LLM judge** | Reads the whole conversation and checks a behavior you describe. Best for refusals, steps, and policy. |
| **Text match** | Checks agent replies for text, ignoring case. Choose **Any reply contains**, **No reply contains**, or **Any reply matches**, which takes a regular expression. Best for IDs and secrets. |
| **Tool calls** | Checks the tools the agent called. List the tools it **Must call** and the tools it **Must never call**. Under **Order and arguments**, choose **Exact order**, **Any order**, **At least these**, or **Only these tools**, and optionally match arguments. |

**Tool calls** assertions need tool call capture. Turn on **Capture tool calls** when you start the simulation. See [Tool call tracing](/tool-tracing).

A scenario can have up to {assertionsPerScenario} assertions, including at most one **Tool calls** assertion. Changes to assertions apply to new simulations.

Each conversation's result appears when you run an evaluation. See [Evaluations](/evaluations).

## Add scenarios

Open a group and click **Add Scenarios**. Choose one of three options:

| Option | Best when |
| --- | --- |
| **Build with Assistant** | You want to describe what to test and let an assistant draft the scenarios. |
| **From Conversation History** | You have real conversation logs and want scenarios that match how your users actually behave. |
| **Import a File** | You already have scenarios in a JSON or CSV file. |

Every option saves scenarios into the group you opened.

<Frame>
  ![The Add Scenarios dialog with three options](/images/add-scenarios-picker.png)
</Frame>

### Build with the assistant

Choose **Build with Assistant**. The assistant asks you a few questions about your agent, then drafts scenarios you can review before you save them. The workspace has a chat on the left and two tabs on the right: **Plan** and **Scenarios**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe your agent">
    Tell the assistant what your agent does and what you want to test. You can also paste a link to a web page, and the assistant reads it and attaches it as a source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the goals">
    The assistant proposes the user goals to test and how many scenarios to write for each. It also suggests paths where things go wrong. Adjust the list, then click **Send answers**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose knowledge">
    Pick what the scenarios are grounded in:

    - **My whole knowledge base**: searched for each goal, including anything you add later.
    - **Choose documents**: pick specific items. Search, or filter by tag.
    - **Pasted records**: paste the orders, accounts, or policies your agent holds.
    - **Nothing**: the scenarios exercise the conversation only.

    Only knowledge items with the **Success** status are listed. See [Knowledge](/knowledge).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe your customers">
    Choose **Describe your customer segments** to paste your own research, or click **Suggest some for me**. You can also choose **None**. Next, add any attributes that should vary between simulated users, such as plan type or age.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate">
    Click **Generate** to write the scenarios. One run writes at most {scenariosPerRun} scenarios. To write more, click **Generate** again. Click **Stop** to cancel a run in progress.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and save">
    The drafts appear on the **Scenarios** tab, already selected. Review them, clear any you don't want, and click **Save N selected**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Building scenarios uses your plan's token allowance. If the allowance is used up, the assistant shows a **Go to billing** link. See [Settings](/settings#billing).
</Note>

#### Edit the plan

The **Plan** tab shows everything the assistant will use: knowledge, agent context, goals with their scenario counts, **Customer segments (optional)**, and **Persona attributes (optional)**. Edit any of them directly, then click **Save plan**. Set a goal's count to 0 to skip it. You can also click **Change the split** above the generate button to adjust the counts.

#### Review drafts

Each draft card shows the goal, the persona, the user goal checklist, the assertions, and the knowledge it is grounded in. Use the icons on a card to edit, regenerate, or discard it.

The assistant also checks the drafts and flags problems on the card, for example a duplicate, a contradiction with your knowledge, or a goal that gives away the outcome. If you save a selection that includes flagged drafts, Arklex asks you to save again to confirm.

If you didn't attach any knowledge, a warning says the scenarios will be invented rather than grounded in your documents. Click **Choose knowledge** to add some, or continue with **Generate N anyway**.

You can also ask the assistant in the chat to change specific drafts, for example "make the second customer less patient". Or ask it to review the scenarios already saved in the group for duplicates and gaps.

#### Choose a model

The session header shows the model the assistant uses. Choose a different model before you send your first message. After the session starts, the model is fixed. To switch, click **Start over**. Scenarios you already saved are kept.

#### Leave and come back

The assistant saves your conversation as you go, and generation keeps running if you leave the page. Only you can see your unsaved sessions.

To return, click **History** in the session header, or open the group's **Drafts** tab. The **Drafts** tab lists your unsaved sessions in the group. Each row shows how the session started and how many drafts are saved and unsaved. Click a row to continue where you left off. Saved scenarios move to the **Scenarios** tab.

Groups created before the assistant existed may also show **Unfinished runs from earlier generation methods** on the **Drafts** tab. You can't continue these runs.

### Build from conversation history

Choose **From Conversation History** to start the same assistant session, but first reads your past conversations. The assistant proposes goals, scenario counts, customer segments, and attributes based on what it finds. Click **Use it as it is** to accept the proposal, or **Adjust the counts** to change it. The session then continues with the knowledge question.

Upload a `.json` or `.jsonl` file of up to {historyUploadMb} MB:

- **JSON**: a list of conversations.
- **JSONL**: one conversation per line.

Each conversation needs a `messages` list. Each message needs a `role` (`user`, `assistant`, or `bot`) and its `content`:

```json
[
  {
    "messages": [
      { "role": "user", "content": "I want to dispute a charge on my card." },
      { "role": "assistant", "content": "I can help with that. Which charge?" }
    ]
  }
]
```

Reading a large file takes a few minutes. It keeps going if you leave the page.

This option is most useful once you have production traffic and want your scenarios to reflect the real variety of users talking to your agent.

### Import a file

Choose **Import a File** to upload scenarios you already have. The import has three steps: **Upload**, **Edit**, and **Review**.

1. **Upload**: select a `.json` or `.csv` file of up to {importFileMb} MB. Click **Download template** for an example file.
2. **Edit**: review the parsed scenarios. Fix errors, add or delete scenarios, and edit fields.
3. **Review**: check the summary and click **Import N Scenarios**. Rows with errors are skipped.

Each scenario needs a goal, a user profile, and an agent context. The other fields are optional.

| Field | Required | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `name` | No | Scenario name, up to {scenarioNameChars} characters. |
| `goal` | Yes | What the simulated user wants, up to {goalChars} characters. |
| `user_profile` | Yes | Who the simulated user is, up to {userProfileChars} characters. |
| `agent_context` | Yes | What the agent is and does, up to {agentContextChars} characters. |
| `knowledge` | No | Up to {knowledgePerScenario} knowledge passages. |
| `goal_checklist` | No | Up to {checklistItems} user goal checklist items. |
| `assertions` | No | The scenario's [assertions](#assertions). JSON files only. |

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="JSON">
    A JSON file is a list of scenarios, or an object with the list under `scenarios`, `items`, or `data`. Each knowledge passage has a `name` and `content`. Each checklist item has a `description`, an optional `satisfied_when`, and `mandatory`.

    ```json
    {
      "scenarios": [
        {
          "name": "Lost card replacement",
          "goal": "You lost your debit card yesterday and want it blocked and replaced.",
          "user_profile": "You are a busy nurse who wants this sorted quickly.",
          "agent_context": "A banking assistant for a retail bank.",
          "knowledge": [
            { "name": "Card policy", "content": "Lost cards are blocked right away. Replacements arrive in 5 business days." }
          ],
          "goal_checklist": [
            { "description": "Agent blocks the card", "mandatory": true },
            { "description": "Agent says when the replacement arrives", "mandatory": false }
          ],
          "assertions": [
            { "type": "llm_judge", "expected": "The agent verifies the customer's identity before blocking the card." },
            { "type": "text_match", "op": "not_contains", "value": "full card number" },
            {
              "type": "tool_calls",
              "match_mode": "contains",
              "expected": [{ "name": "block_card" }],
              "forbidden": [{ "name": "close_account" }]
            }
          ]
        }
      ]
    }
    ```

    Each assertion has a `type` and the fields for that kind:

    | `type` | Fields |
    | --- | --- |
    | `llm_judge` | `expected`: the behavior to check. |
    | `text_match` | `op` (`contains`, `not_contains`, or `matches`) and `value`. |
    | `tool_calls` | `expected` and `forbidden`: lists of tools by `name`. `match_mode`: `strict` (Exact order), `unordered` (Any order), `contains` (At least these), or `within` (Only these tools). To check arguments, add `arguments` and `arg_match_mode` (`partial` or `exact`) to a tool. |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CSV">
    A CSV file has one scenario per row, with the field names above as column headers.

    - **Knowledge**: use `knowledge_name` and `knowledge_content` for the first passage. For more, add `_2`, `_3`, and so on, for example `knowledge_name_2`, up to {knowledgePerScenario} passages.
    - **Checklist**: use `checklist_description`, `checklist_satisfied_when`, and `checklist_mandatory` for the first item. For more, add `_2`, `_3`, and so on, up to {checklistItems} items.

    CSV files can't include assertions. Add them after the import, or use a JSON file.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Manage scenarios

**Edit**: open the scenario and click **Edit**. Change any field, then click **Save**. If your change affects generated fields, such as the checklist or assertions, Arklex asks whether to update them too. Choose **Save and regenerate** or **Save without regenerating**. If you edit the user profile, click **Sync attributes** to update the attributes to match.

**Versions**: each save that changes something creates a new version. Use the **Version** menu at the top of the scenario to view earlier versions. Earlier versions are read-only. Simulations record which version of each scenario they used, so past results stay accurate.

**Delete**: in the group's **Scenarios** tab, click the trash icon on the scenario's row and confirm. You can't delete a scenario that a simulation has used.

To choose scenarios for a simulation, see [Create a simulation](/simulations#create-a-simulation).

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How many scenarios should I start with?">
    Start with 10 to 15 scenarios covering your most important user intents. Once you've reviewed the results, run a new simulation with a broader set.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need knowledge to build scenarios?">
    No. Without knowledge, the assistant invents the details, which is fine for testing conversation flow. Grounded scenarios are better for checking whether your agent gets the facts right.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What makes a good scenario goal?">
    Specific enough to drive a focused conversation, but not so prescriptive that it removes all variation:

    - **Too vague:** "Talk to the agent about your account."
    - **Too prescriptive:** "Say 'I want to dispute charge #12345 on account #67890 from 2024-01-15 and I expect a refund within 3 days.'"
    - **Good:** "You spotted a \$240 charge you don't recognize on last month's statement and want it investigated and refunded."
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
