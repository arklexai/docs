This guide takes you from your first sign-in to your first evaluation, in one path. It follows one running example: a **Banking Assistant** that helps customers check balances, make transfers, and resolve card issues. Every step works the same for any conversational agent you connect.

Each step uses the defaults. For every field and option, follow the links to the reference pages.

## Before you start

- An Arklex Platform account. If your team already has an organization, ask one of its Owners to invite you, so you join it directly. Otherwise, choose **Sign Up** when you first open Arklex Platform.
- An agent with an HTTP endpoint that Arklex can reach. On cloud, that means a public URL. See [Agents](/agents) for the supported API types.
- Any auth headers your endpoint requires, for example an API key or bearer token.

On your first sign-in, Arklex asks you to complete your profile and shows a short walkthrough. The **Onboarding Checklist** in the left sidebar then tracks the steps in this guide, plus setting up metrics.

---

## Step 1: Connect an agent

Go to **Agents** in the left sidebar and click **Connect Agent**. Enter an **Agent Name**, for example `Banking Assistant`. Choose the **API Type**, paste the **Endpoint URL**, and add any auth headers your endpoint needs. If your endpoint expects a request body, set it up as described in [Configure the request body](/agents#configure-the-request-body).

Click **Test Connection** to check that the endpoint responds, then click **Connect Agent** to save.

**You'll know it worked when** the test shows **Agent responded successfully** and the agent's detail page opens.

## Step 2: Add scenarios

A scenario describes one kind of conversation to test: who the simulated user is, what they want, and the assertions that decide whether your agent passes. You build scenarios with an assistant that asks you a few questions and then drafts them. See [How a scenario works](/scenarios#how-a-scenario-works).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add knowledge (optional)">
    Knowledge grounds scenarios in your real content, and the judge checks your agent's answers against it. Go to **Knowledge** and add websites or upload documents. For this example, upload the bank's policy and FAQ documents. Wait until each item shows **Success**.

    <video controls muted playsInline preload="metadata" title="Uploading bank documents to the knowledge base" src="/videos/add-knowledge.mp4" style={{ width: "100%", aspectRatio: "1.6", borderRadius: "8px" }} />
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a group">
    Go to **Scenarios**, click **Create group**, name it (for example `Core Support`), and click **Create**. The group opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the assistant">
    Click **Add Scenarios**, then choose **Build with Assistant**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe your agent">
    Tell the assistant what your agent does and what you want to test. For example: "A banking assistant for a retail bank. Customers check balances, make transfers, and resolve card issues."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer the assistant's questions">
    Confirm the goals to test and how many scenarios to write for each, then click **Send answers**. Next, choose the knowledge to use (select **My whole knowledge base** for this example). Then describe your customers, or choose **None**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate and save">
    Click **Generate N** to write the scenarios. Each draft comes with a user goal checklist and assertions. Review the drafts on the **Scenarios** tab, edit any assertion that doesn't match what your agent should do, then click **Save N selected**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**You'll know it worked when** your saved scenarios appear on the group's **Scenarios** tab.

## Step 3: Run a simulation

A simulation runs your scenarios against the connected agent and records full conversation transcripts. Go to **Simulations** and click **New Simulation**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select agent">
    Select the agent you connected in Step 1.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the simulation">
    Give it a descriptive name, for example "Banking Assistant transfers and card support".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select scenarios">
    Choose the scenarios to include.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the defaults">
    Leave the other settings as they are. See [Create a simulation](/simulations#create-a-simulation) for what each one does.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run">
    Click **Run Simulation**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The new simulation appears at the top of the list. Click it to open the detail page. Each row in the conversations table is one simulated conversation. Click any row to read the full transcript.

**You'll know it worked when** the simulation status reaches **Completed** and its conversations show **Done**.

## Step 4: Run an evaluation and review results

An evaluation grades each conversation with its scenario's assertions, and scores it on the metrics you pick with an LLM judge. Once the simulation shows **Completed**, go to **Evaluations** and click **New Evaluation**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose what to evaluate">
    Leave **Evaluate** set to **Agent**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the evaluation">
    Enter an **Evaluation Name**, for example "Core Support baseline".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select simulation">
    Select the simulation you just ran.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the default metrics and model">
    **Helpfulness**, **Coherence**, and **Relevance** are selected, along with a default judge model. See [Create an evaluation](/evaluations#create-an-evaluation) for the other options.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run the evaluation">
    Click **Run Evaluation**. The status changes to **Completed** when grading is done.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the results">
    Click the completed evaluation. The **Overview** tab shows how many conversations passed, the metric scores, and the failure patterns that recur across conversations.

    Click a conversation in the **Failed** list. Its **Assertions** section, above the transcript, shows which check failed and why. See [Read an evaluation's results](/evaluations#read-an-evaluations-results).
  </Step>
</Steps>

**You'll know it worked when** you can point to a specific finding, for example "the agent discussed account details before verifying the customer's identity", and trace it back to the turns that triggered it.

## Next steps

Your first evaluation is the loop you'll repeat as your agent evolves. From here:

- [Invite your team](/settings) so reviewers can add **annotations** and calibrate judge scores against human judgment.
- [Add assertions](/scenarios#assertions), such as **Text match** and **Tool calls**, to define exactly what a passing conversation looks like.
- [Add more agents](/agents) to compare versions or test different prompts and models against the same scenarios.
- [Customize metrics](/metrics) to score behaviors specific to your domain.
- [Capture tool calls](/tool-tracing) to see and check the tools your agent calls.
