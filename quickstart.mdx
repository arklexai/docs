This guide takes you from your first sign-in to your first evaluation. It follows one running example: a **Banking Assistant** that helps customers check balances, make transfers, and resolve card issues. Every step works the same for any conversational agent you connect.

Each step maps directly to a page in the platform, so the workflow you learn here is the workflow you'll use every day.

## Before you start

- An Arklex Platform account. If your team already has an organization, ask one of its Owners to invite you, so you join it directly. Otherwise, choose **Sign Up** when you first open Arklex Platform.
- An agent with an HTTP endpoint that the public internet can reach. Arklex supports **Chat Completions** endpoints, the **A2A** protocol, and any JSON endpoint through **Custom HTTP**.
- Any auth headers your endpoint requires, for example an API key or bearer token.

On your first sign-in, Arklex asks you to complete your profile and shows a short walkthrough. The **Onboarding Checklist** in the left sidebar then tracks the steps in this guide.

## Step 1: Connect an agent

Go to **Agents** in the left sidebar and click **Connect Agent**.

Fill in the form:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the agent">
    Enter a descriptive **Agent Name**, for example `Banking Assistant`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the API type">
    Select **Chat Completions** for most agents, **Agent to Agent (A2A)** for the A2A protocol, or **Custom HTTP** for any other JSON endpoint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the endpoint URL">
    Paste your agent's endpoint. For A2A, enter the agent's base URL instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add auth headers">
    Add any headers your endpoint requires, for example `Authorization: Bearer <token>`. Header values are encrypted at rest.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the body">
    For Chat Completions, add the parameters and messages your endpoint expects. For Custom HTTP, write a request body template. A2A agents don't use a body.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Click **Test Connection** to check that the endpoint responds, then click **Connect Agent** to save.

**You'll know it worked when** the test shows **Agent responded successfully** and the agent's detail page opens.

## Step 2: Add scenarios

A scenario represents one type of simulated user: who they are, what they want, and what they know. You build scenarios with an assistant that asks you a few questions and then drafts them.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add knowledge (optional)">
    Knowledge grounds scenarios in your real content, so simulated users ask realistic, specific questions. Go to **Knowledge** and add websites or upload documents. For this example, upload the bank's policy and FAQ documents. Wait until each item shows **Success**.

    <video controls muted playsInline preload="metadata" title="Uploading bank documents to the knowledge base" src="/videos/add-knowledge.mp4" style={{ width: "100%", aspectRatio: "1.6", borderRadius: "8px" }} />
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a group">
    Go to **Scenarios**, click **Create group**, name it (for example `Core Support`), and click **Create**. Then open the group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the assistant">
    Click **Add Scenarios**, then choose **Build with Assistant**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe your agent">
    Tell the assistant what your agent does and what you want to test. For example: "A banking assistant for a retail bank. Customers check balances, make transfers, and resolve card issues."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer the assistant's questions">
    Confirm the goals to test and how many scenarios to write for each, then click **Send answers**. Next, choose the knowledge to use (select **My whole knowledge base** for this example). Then describe your customers, or choose **None**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate and save">
    Click **Generate** to write the scenarios. Review the drafts on the **Scenarios** tab, then click **Save N selected**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**You'll know it worked when** your saved scenarios appear on the group's **Scenarios** tab.

## Step 3: Run a simulation

A simulation runs your scenarios against the connected agent and records full conversation transcripts. Go to **Simulations** and click **New Simulation**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select agent">
    Select the agent you connected in Step 1.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the simulation">
    Give it a descriptive name, for example "Banking Assistant transfers and card support".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select scenarios">
    Choose the scenarios to include.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure the conversations">
    Set **Conversations per scenario** (default {conversationsPerScenarioDefault}, max {conversationsPerScenarioMax}) and **Max turns per conversation** (default {maxTurnsDefault}, max {maxTurnsMax}). Leave **Max concurrent conversations** at its default unless your agent struggles under load.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run">
    Click **Run Simulation**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The new simulation appears at the top of the list. Click it to open the detail page. Each row in the conversations table is one simulated conversation. Click any row to read the full transcript.

**You'll know it worked when** the simulation status reaches **Completed** and its conversations show **Done**.

## Step 4: Run an evaluation and review results

An evaluation checks each conversation against its scenario's assertions and scores it with an LLM judge. Once the simulation shows **Completed**, go to **Evaluations** and click **New Evaluation**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose what to evaluate">
    Leave **Evaluate** set to **Agent**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the evaluation">
    Enter an **Evaluation Name**, for example "Core Support baseline".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select simulation">
    Select the simulation you just ran.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select metrics">
    **Helpfulness**, **Coherence**, and **Relevance** are selected by default. Add any other built-in or custom metrics you care about.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a model">
    Pick the **Provider** and **Model** the judge uses, or keep the default.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run the evaluation">
    Click **Run Evaluation**. The status changes to **Completed** when scoring is done.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the results">
    Click the completed evaluation to open its detail page:

    - **Quantitative Metrics** opens with **Conversations passed**, the share of conversations whose assertions all passed. Below it, each metric shows its average score with a band label (Excellent, Good, Needs Improvement, or Poor).
    - **Qualitative Metrics** shows label distributions for categorical metrics.
    - **Unique Errors** lists failed assertions and behavioral failures found by the judge.
    - **Conversations** lists every conversation with its **Result** (Pass or Fail) and how many assertions passed.

    Click any conversation row to open the transcript. Expand **Assertions** to see why each check passed or failed, and click **metrics** under an agent turn to see that turn's scores.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**You'll know it worked when** you can point to a specific finding, for example "the agent discussed account details before verifying the customer's identity", and trace it back to the turns that triggered it.
